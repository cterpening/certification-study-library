# Places to learn

This catalog complements the study guides with several learning formats. It is a curated starting point, not a complete list of worthwhile instructors, courses, books, labs, or providers. It does not rank one learning style as universally best, and inclusion is not an endorsement of every course sold by a provider.

Do not treat this page as a completion checklist. You are not expected to consume all of this content. Pick the official material, explanations, instructor, format, and hands-on practice that work for you, then use the current exam blueprint to identify and close your remaining gaps.

**Last reviewed:** August 31, 2026

Course catalogs, prices, access models, and course content change. Always compare a resource with the current official exam blueprint before using it as a study plan.

## How to read the time estimates

Each guide's final `Places to learn` table estimates the time needed to consume that resource at normal speed. A provider-published runtime or course duration is used when available. Otherwise the table says `about`, gives a range, or tells you to verify the current provider runtime. Reading estimates assume active reading, not merely page count.

These are not total preparation-time promises. Labs, pausing, note-taking, spaced review, troubleshooting, accessibility needs, and prerequisite gaps add time. Provider course lengths also change. Use the estimates to choose a resource that fits your plan, not to consume everything in the catalog.

## A reliable learning sequence

1. Read the official exam blueprint and use its published objectives as the scope checklist.
2. Complete the matching official learning paths when available.
3. Keep first-party product documentation open for current behavior.
4. Practice in a disposable repository, tenant, subscription, or sandbox appropriate to the technology.
5. Add one course, book, or video series that fits how you learn; add more only when they close a specific gap.
6. Return to the blueprint and explain or demonstrate every objective without notes.

## How resources are selected

A listed resource should have a clear author or publisher, teach concepts rather than claim access to live exam items, and offer useful coverage beyond a question bank. Resources are excluded when they advertise dumps, recalled or “actual” questions, guaranteed passes, or unexplained answer banks.

Third-party resources are evaluated individually. A trustworthy marketplace can still contain an unsuitable or stale course. Ratings and popularity are discovery signals, not evidence that technical claims are correct.

## Contributing a learning resource

When proposing a resource, include the exact course, book, playlist, or repository—not just the provider—and record:

- author or instructor;
- format and access model;
- publication and last-update dates shown by the provider;
- exam objectives it supports;
- strengths, prerequisites, and known gaps;
- any licensing or exam-integrity concern.

Do not copy paid material, transcripts, slides, or proprietary labs. Summarize independently, attribute ideas, and cite current first-party documentation for technical behavior.
